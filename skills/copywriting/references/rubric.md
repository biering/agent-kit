# Scoring a candidate line

Six criteria, 1 to 5 each, 30 points. A line ships at 24, with two hard gates that override the
total.

| # | Criterion | 5 looks like | 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Clarity** (gate) | Ten readers picture the same product | Could describe three different products |
| 2 | **Excitement** | Provokes curiosity, recognition or *wait, what?* | Accurate, inert, forgettable |
| 3 | **Truth** (gate) | Believing it and then seeing the product feels informed | Reader feels tricked; number invented |
| 4 | **Concreteness** | Nouns and verbs you can point at; a number if one exists | Shape words: platform, solution, layer, system, approach |
| 5 | **Budget** | At or under the surface's word or character budget | Over budget, or padded to fill it |
| 6 | **Voice** | The repo's voice and address form, plain, the way a person talks | Corporate-neutral, formalised, translated-sounding |

**Gate 1: clarity below 4 rejects the line**, whatever the total. An unclear line cannot be
exciting, so high excitement next to clarity 3 measures the wrong thing.

**Gate 2: truth below 3 rejects the line.** If it got there by inventing a number or a claim, say
so explicitly instead of quietly dropping the candidate.

Reading the total: 27 and up, ship it. 24 to 26, ship after one more pass at the weakest
criterion. 20 to 23, the idea may be right but this sentence is not; generate again from the
brief, not from this line. Below 20, the brief is wrong: go back to Frame.

Two failure modes this rubric catches:

- **The safe line.** Clarity 5, truth 5, excitement 2, total 24: a pass on paper, and a caption.
  Say so in the proposal and show the bolder candidate next to it with what would have to be
  proven to ship it.
- **The clever line.** Excitement 5, clarity 3: rejected by gate 1 however much the room enjoyed
  it.
