# Copy blocks

One recipe per block: its job, its budget, what goes wrong. A page is a sequence of these picked
by the format ([formats.md](formats.md)). Budgets are for English; German runs about a fifth
longer.

Write the hero last. It compresses everything below it, so it comes after the rest exists.

## Header

Logo left, the CTA right. On mobile the CTA shrinks to one word ("Start"). Sticky. No links.
The logo does not link to the homepage: that is a way off the page.

## Hero

**Headline.** Job: the reader recognises the ad they just clicked and their own situation, in
that order. Budget: 6 to 12 words, at most two lines on a phone.

- Repeat the ad's promise with the ad's own nouns. If the ad said "Excel", the headline says
  "Excel", not "spreadsheet".
- Build it on a noun the reader owns (your listing, your spreadsheet, your data room) and a verb
  that is a fact about their week.
- The product name comes last or not at all.

Patterns that hold up, with the slot that changes per angle:

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Gap | Your [A] does not know your [B]. [Brand] does. | Your listing does not know your bank's rate. Acme does. |
| Swap | [Result] in [new time] instead of [old time]. | The analysis in 2 minutes instead of an evening. |
| Missing piece | [Familiar thing] shows you [X]. Not [Y]. | Your spreadsheet shows the return. Not the risk. |
| The reader's own question | [Their question]? | Did the agent even open your offer? |
| Deadline | [What happens] before [date]. | Read the data room before you sign. |

**Subline.** Job: say how, in one or two sentences, with the mechanism and the outcome. Budget:
25 words. It answers "how would that work?" and nothing else. No second promise.

**CTA.** A verb, the object, the price of the first step. "Analyse your first listing free"
beats "Get started", because it says what happens after the click. One wording across the page.
A second wording is a second action.

**Risk line** next to or under the button: three checkable facts separated by a middle dot
("No credit card · EU hosting · 2 minutes"). Leave out any you cannot prove from code or contract.

**Proof number.** One figure with its unit and a label, and only a real one, with its query and
date in the claim ledger. A page without a counter loses nothing; a page with an invented one
loses the account when someone asks.

## Proof strip ("Works with")

Job: remove "does it work with what I use?". A row of names the reader already trusts: the
portals, the file types, the sources. Names only, with small generic icons. Third-party logos need
permission we usually lack: use the name in text.

## Demo

Job: let the reader see the result before they sign up. Three shapes, strongest first:

1. A live input on the page (paste a link, get a result).
2. A drawn result with real numbers from one worked case.
3. A chat exchange: the reader's question, the product's answer, three figures.

Every number in a demo comes from one consistent case that someone calculated. Mixed numbers from
three cases are noticed by exactly the readers you want. Label it as an example when it is not a
customer's case.

Tabs by audience only when the ad set targets several audiences with one page. The tab matching
the ad angle is open on load.

## Problem ("The gap")

Job: name what the reader already suspects. Two cards, never five. Each card:

- a bold sentence of at most eight words that states the problem as a fact,
- two sentences of how it shows up in their week,
- three chips naming the concrete things involved (documents, tools, people).

Write it from their side of the table: what they do, what it costs them, what they end up not
knowing. The product stays out of this block.

## Before/After

Job: turn the promise into arithmetic. Two columns, four rows, a duration per row and a total per
column.

- Left rows are steps the reader really takes, in the order they take them.
- Right rows are what the product does, each with a duration you measured.
- Totals in mono type. Red for the left total, green for the right.
- On mobile it is a two-segment toggle that opens on "with".

A duration is a claim. It goes in the ledger with how it was measured. Unmeasured: write the step
with no time, or use a bounded phrase the product guarantees ("live", "right after upload").

## Steps ("How it works")

Three steps. Each: a timestamp or number, a title of at most four words, one sentence, up to two
status chips. The third step is the reader deciding or acting, not the product finishing: the
page ends on what they get to do.

## Objections

Three to five, as questions in the reader's words, each answered in one or two sentences that
start with the answer. Take them from sales calls, support mail and the comments under the ads,
not from what you wish they would ask. The usual five:

1. What does it really cost? (the first step, then the plan)
2. What happens to my documents? (where stored, who sees them, deletion)
3. Are the numbers right? (source of each figure, what is estimated)
4. Do I need X for this? (prior knowledge, a subscription, a download)
5. What if it does not work for my case? (the limit and the refund)

An honest limit here raises conversion with the readers worth having: say what the product does
not do.

## Closing CTA

Job: one more chance for the reader who read to the end. The headline restates the hero from the
other side: the hero named the problem, the close names the first step. One sentence under it
with the risk line as prose. The button, in the same wording.

## Footer

Copyright, the legal links and the brand's mandatory note (repo doc). Nothing else.

## Sticky CTA (mobile)

A bar at the bottom with the one button, shown once the hero CTA has left the viewport, hidden
while the closing CTA is in view. The page gets bottom padding of the bar's height so the bar
never covers the footer.

## Sentence rules

- The brand's language and form of address (repo doc).
- One thought per sentence. A sentence that needs a comma and an "and" is two.
- Numbers as numerals, with the unit, in the page language's format.
- No em dashes: period, comma or colon.
- No superlatives and no grading adjectives ("easy", "smart", "revolutionary", "effortless").
  Replace each with the fact that made you want to write it.
- The reader is the subject of most sentences. Count the sentences that start with the brand
  name: more than one in five means the page is about us.
- Every block survives being read alone. Ad traffic scrolls, stops, and reads one block.
