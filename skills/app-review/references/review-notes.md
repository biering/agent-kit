# Review notes

The reader is smart and busy and has never heard of your app. Write for a total
beginner: short bullets, plain words, no marketing, no jargon, no idea that
sounds machine-written. Concrete beats clever. Aim for under a page. Paste the
result into App Review Information > Notes, and keep a copy in the app's
distribution folder so the next submission starts from it.

## The fixed format

Use these six headings in this order. Leave a heading out only if it truly does
not apply, and then say "none" rather than dropping it.

```text
What the app does
- One sentence: who it is for and what they do in it.
- Version <x.y>: what is new in this version, in one or two bullets.

How to reach each feature
- Feature name: tap <tab or button>, then <next step>. One line each.
- Sign-in: whether it is needed. If yes, the demo account (email only here; the password goes in the dedicated field).
- Anything behind a setting or a gesture: where it is.

Paywall and how to test the purchase
- Where the paywall appears and what it unlocks.
- What stays free.
- Product name and type (for example one-time unlock or subscription).
- To test: use a sandbox account, tap <button>, confirm; the feature unlocks. Restore: <where the button is>.
- The review screenshot on the purchase shows this same screen.

Data we collect and why
- What leaves the device, when, and why. Say "nothing" if nothing.
- Anything optional: how it is asked, that "No" is equal to "Yes" in weight, where to turn it off.
- Permissions asked, when, and what each is for.
- Where the privacy policy is.

Anything unusual
- Anything a reviewer might flag, said first by you. Health-adjacent features and what they do not claim. A pattern we deliberately did not ship yet. Hardware or region limits. Background modes and why.
- Hardware or accounts needed to test, and how to get around the need.

Demo video
- One line: "A screen recording with captions for each section is attached."
```

## Style rules

- One fact per bullet. Verbs first: "Tap Settings, then Share anonymous usage."
- Name things exactly as the screen does.
- No "seamless", "powerful", "revolutionary", no "we are excited".
- No em dashes. No rule-of-three lists for effect.
- Do not apologise and do not argue in notes. State what is there.
- If something looks like a violation but is not, say why in one line, with the guideline number.
- Never claim the app is perfect. Say what you tested.

## Test it

Hand the notes to someone who has never seen the app. If they cannot find every
feature, the notes are not done.
