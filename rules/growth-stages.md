# Growth stages

Which growth work fits a product at which revenue. A creator campaign is wasted on a product at $0
that has no customers yet, and hand sales stop scaling at $50k a month. Read the stage
before planning distribution work, and do the work of that stage and the ones below it. A repo maps
the stages onto its own distribution system and names its own channels; this file only says when.

**The stage is measured, never guessed:** paid revenue in the last 30 days, sales recorded by hand
and store proceeds included (MRR for a subscription), in USD or the same amount in the repo's
currency. A product moves up when it held the next stage's floor for two months in a row and you
can name the channel that brought most of it. Write the stage where agents plan from, with the
figure, its source and the date, and read it before picking distribution work.
It never skips a stage, and the work of a lower stage keeps running.

## The stages

| Stage | Revenue a month | Run | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Idea** | Nothing built | The demand test (below) | Building, launches, accounts |
| **1. First customers** | $0 to $5k | The first 10 customers by hand; borrowed reach (partners, other people's threads and audiences); every launch platform and directory that fits; a free tool; search foundations | Creators, sponsorships, ads beyond a capped test, an open affiliate program, hiring |
| **2. Repeatable** | $5k to $20k | Search and content every week; trigger outreach where the law allows; the small features users love, each marketed as its own launch; more free tools; an affiliate or referral program with a recurring cut | Creators, sponsorships, ads beyond a capped test |
| **3. More channels** | $20k to $50k | What worked keeps running. Customer wins as stories; rewards for users who post about you; communities; integrations, marketplaces, plugins; one new channel at a time; bigger plans (teams, volume) | Creators, sponsorships, ads beyond a capped test |
| **4. Amplify** | $50k to $100k | Creator content (AI first, then real creators); sponsored newsletters, podcasts and channels the buyers follow; a price raise | Ads beyond a capped test |
| **5. Scale** | $100k and up | Paid ads at scale, with the best creator content as the creative; one owner (a person or a dedicated agent) for the best channel | |

"Creators" are people paid to post about the product; the product's own accounts and the content
it makes for them are owned reach and run at any stage. A capped ad test (a fixed small sum to
learn what a click or a sale costs) counts as measurement and may run from stage 1.

## Rules for every stage

- **The demand test comes before the build.** Show the idea where its buyers are (a post, a
  landing with a waitlist, a pre-order) and write down a number of real signals and a window first,
  for example 10 strangers who ask for it within 7 days. A signal is a stranger who acts: a message
  asking for it, a call booked, a deposit or a pre-order. Likes, views and compliments are not.
  Miss it and the idea is killed or the angle changes; it never ships by default.
- **The first 10 customers are won by hand.** Agents build the list (people who said they have the
  problem, in public or to you), the context per person and the message; the owner sends it, calls,
  and answers. Every message names why this person, now: they posted about the problem, launched,
  hired, raised. No list bought, no blast.
- **One new channel at a time, 60 days**, with its signal and kill rule written before it starts.
  Keep the winner, kill the rest, write down why.
- **The 90-day read**, from the first paying customers on: where did the last 90 days of paying
  customers come from? Double down on the top source and stop the channels that brought none.
  Judge by profit.
- **A price review** at every stage change and at least every 90 days: price page conversion,
  revenue per plan, what buyers asked for. One change at a time, as an experiment: a higher price
  or a bigger plan. The owner picks the number.
- **A recurring cut, written down.** Affiliates and referrers get a share of what the customer
  keeps paying (20 to 40% for software, set once per product by the owner), a personal code and
  link so sales are counted, and the payout is a cost the day it is owed.

## Hard lines

- **No invented users, quotes, stories or numbers.** A success story is a real customer, in their
  own words, published with their consent. A thin proof list stays thin.
- **A reward is disclosed.** A user who gets a credit or a free month for a post marks it as an ad
  where the law asks (in Germany "Werbung", in the US per the FTC guides). Never reward a store
  rating or a review, and never buy votes, upvotes, reviews or swaps.
- **The law per market decides the outreach channel.** In Germany (UWG § 7) an advertising email
  needs prior consent, from a business too, and a sales call to a business needs at least presumed
  consent; treat a platform message to a stranger like an email. Other markets have their own
  rules: read them before the first message, not after a complaint.
- **Humans post where the platform demands a human.** Agents draft; the owner posts, under their
  own name, with the disclosure the platform asks for.
