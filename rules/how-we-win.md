# How we win

Guiding principles for a company run by one owner and many agents, condensed from Elon Musk's "How
to win". They decide how ideas are evaluated, how features and products are built, how experiments
run and how the work moves forward. A repo names its own hard lines in its `VISION.md` or
`AGENTS.md`; where a principle meets a hard line, the hard line wins.

## Think from first principles

- **Don't copy what already exists.** Boil a problem down to what you know is true (data with a
  source and a date), then reason up from there.
- "X, but for Y" is analogy: a starting guess, never the reason to build.
- First show that something is possible (a working prototype, one real user, one payment). How
  likely it is to win comes after.

## Simplify, in this order

1. **Question every requirement** and name who made it. A rule from a smart person, a doc or a
   skill is the most dangerous, because nobody questions it. Then make it less dumb.
2. **Delete every part, step, page or feature you can.** If you never add 10% back, you did not
   delete enough.
3. Only then simplify and optimize. Never optimize something that should not exist.
4. Then shorten the cycle time.
5. Automate last. Never speed up or automate a process that should not exist.

## How we operate

- **The only laws of physics are the hard lines:** the law, customer data, money and the owner's
  approvals, and honesty in public (no invented users, quotes or numbers). Everything else, the
  repo's own docs and skills included, is a recommendation to question.
- **A maniacal sense of urgency is the operating principle.** Ship today, not this week. Agents
  run around the clock; work that waits on nobody is waste.
- It is fine to be wrong. Do not be confident and wrong: say what is measured, what is assumed,
  and the date.

## Work hard

- Output compounds: twice the cycles a week gets a year's work done in months. Keep agents on the
  highest-value work and the owner's time on the few calls only they can make.
- **When something is important enough, do it even if the odds are not in your favor**, and say
  the odds.

## People and risk

- The team decides the result: the best models, skills, partners and audience owners you can get.
- Ask for negative feedback (roasts, reviews, real users, friends) and act on it.
- Signal over noise: stop any effort that does not make a product better or bring revenue.
- **Failure is an option. If nothing is failing, you are not innovating enough.** Every
  experiment has a kill rule, and its lesson is written down.
- Take the bold bet now, while it is still cheap.
